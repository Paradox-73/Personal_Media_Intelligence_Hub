## A. Substantive — these affect what you're allowed to conclude

**1. Fold non-independence makes your transfer p-values anti-conservative.** `RepeatedKFold(10,5)` over Games gives 50 folds drawn from **66 items**. Those 50 folds are not 50 independent experiments — each item lands in the test set 5 times, and the folds overlap heavily. A paired Wilcoxon "on per-item absolute errors" is only honest if you've aggregated to *one* error per unique item (n=66) before testing. If the test is run over OOF *rows* (66×5=330) it's pseudo-replication and `p=0.0001` is inflated — and this is the exact failure mode as the benchmark-inversion bug you already caught. The paper never states **n** for the transfer Wilcoxon. State it explicitly (it should be unique items), and add one sentence in §10 acknowledging that repeated-CV folds are correlated, so the p-values bound significance optimistically. This is the single most important methodological gap because the whole headline rests on the noisiest, smallest domain.

**2. You're missing the one control that closes the loop: a label-permutation placebo source.** Your three controls rule out domain-identity leak, a-better-constant, and length artifact. None of them rules out the most parsimonious skeptical read: *Games is the smallest (N=66) and highest-variance (var 1.98) domain, so it benefits from any additional structure, regardless of whether movie taste maps to game taste.* The gold-standard test is a placebo: keep the source features, **shuffle the source ratings**, re-run the grid. If shuffled-movie still lifts Games ~+0.12, the effect is variance reduction, not taste transfer, and the centerpiece collapses. If it goes to zero, you've nailed it and the finding gets dramatically stronger. The prior-vs-transfer control (Spearman +0.47) gestures at this but doesn't kill it — beating a constant prior isn't the same as beating a label-permuted source. I'd run this before calling the project done.

**3. Zero-shot (−0.19) and augmented-at-fraction-0 (+0.15) cannot both be "source-only on target."** Figure 11 says movie→game zero-shot skill is **−0.19**. Figure 12's Games panel and the prose say the augmented curve sits at **~+0.15 at zero target data**. At fraction 0 there *is* no target data, so "augmented" and "zero-shot" are the same model (movies only, evaluated on games) — yet they disagree in sign. The caption's "they answer different questions" is a hand-wave; at fraction=0 they answer the *same* question. The only reconciliations are (a) the augmented model at fraction 0 isn't actually source-only (it carries the games-mean intercept or CORAL fit on target features), or (b) target-alone skill at fraction 0 is strongly negative and the +0.15 is a *lift over* an even-worse baseline, not an absolute skill. Either way you need a precise operational definition of what the fraction-0 augmented model sees. As printed, your two centerpiece transfer figures contradict each other on sign, and a reviewer will stop here.

**4. The edge-loss section reaches the wrong conclusion — and it contradicts your own earlier diagnosis.** §5 / Fig 8 / the Recommendation say "the mechanism works as designed," "the design is sound but the operating point conservative." That's incorrect, and you already knew it: W(r) keys on **|true-rating| extremity**, not error. It's sample-importance-weighting of edge-*labeled* rows, symmetric in error direction. It cannot do what it's advertised to do ("penalize confident wrongness at extremes") because a model that nails a 5★ gets its (near-zero) error up-weighted to no effect, while a catastrophic miss at r=3 gets W=1. The honest framing is **mis-specified objective**, not "sound design, conservative α." That changes the future-work line from "retune α" to "reformulate as error-magnitude-and-direction-aware." Right now the paper retires the loss for the *wrong reason* (Optuna drove α small) instead of the *right* one (the formulation can't target the phenomenon). Fix the Recommendation and the Fig 8 caption.

**5. "Geometry does not predict transfer" is an overclaim — it's underpowered, not null.** ρ=+0.17 at **n=12** pairs has a 95% CI of roughly ±0.55; you cannot distinguish it from a moderate positive *or* negative association. The sign literally flipped (−0.18 → +0.17) on a data correction over the same 12 pairs — which is direct evidence the estimate is unstable, not that it's a clean zero. Yet contribution #2 (page 2) and abstract finding (2) both state it as an established null. Reframe to "no detectable feature-geometry association at n=12 (inconclusive)" everywhere it appears as a headline. As written it's the weakest claim dressed as a finding.

**6. Your headline config and your multiple-comparisons-surviving config are different.** The abstract headlines **movie→game** (+0.127, parsimonious). The Bonferroni rescue in §7 is computed for **movie+tv+book→game** (p<0.0001 over ~120 cells). You never show that the *headline* config clears correction — only "4/4 significant" at the raw threshold. Report movie→game's per-fraction p-values so the parsimonious claim survives FDR/Bonferroni on its own. Otherwise you're headlining config A and defending config B.

**7. Lift magnitudes have no uncertainty attached.** You report significance (paired Wilcoxon) but never a CI on +0.127 itself. Given the fold structure in #1, +0.127 ± what? Add bootstrap CIs over the lift, or at minimum the fold-level SD.
